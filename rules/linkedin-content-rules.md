# LinkedIn Content Rules: Context for the Content Team

Every Bot reads this file before every task. It explains how LinkedIn content works, what performs, and the rules the team never breaks. Where this file and the owner's own data disagree, the owner's data wins.

---

## 1. How LinkedIn content works

- **Reach is earned in the first hour.** LinkedIn shows a new post to a small slice of the owner's network first. If that group stops scrolling, reads, comments and reposts, the post is shown to more people. If they don't, it dies. So the hook and the first hour decide almost everything.
- **Dwell time matters.** Posts people stop on and read (or swipe through, for carousels) get pushed further. Short lines, white space and a strong creative keep people on the post.
- **Comments beat likes.** A thoughtful comment is worth far more than a like. Comments that start conversations (replies to replies) keep a post alive for days.
- **The feed is people-first.** Personal profiles get far more reach than company pages. Content should sound like a person talking, not a brand announcing.
- **Relevance over virality.** A post that reaches 2,000 of the right buyers beats one that reaches 200,000 of the wrong people. Every post should be interesting to the owner's ideal customer, not just to everyone.
- **Links in the post body tend to cut reach.** LinkedIn keeps people on LinkedIn. Put links in the comments or send them by DM, not in the post itself.
- **Consistency compounds.** Posting 3 to 5 times a week for months beats posting daily for two weeks and stopping. The algorithm and the audience both reward people who show up.

## 2. What a high-performing post looks like

**The structure:**
1. **Hook (line 1):** the only line most people read. It must stop the scroll.
2. **Bracket line (line 2):** a short line in brackets that adds a reason to keep reading. Example: "(here's the exact system)".
3. **Body:** short lines, one idea each, lots of white space. Numbered steps, arrows (→) and simple lists work well.
4. **Proof:** one or two real numbers, results or examples.
5. **Payoff:** the takeaway or the resource.
6. **CTA:** one simple ask.

**Hook patterns that work:**
- **Big result + how:** "I booked 40 calls last month from one LinkedIn profile. (here's the setup)"
- **Contrarian:** "You don't need more followers. You need better DMs."
- **Number + list:** "7 AI tools that replaced my $4K/month marketing team"
- **Mistake:** "Most founders raise money in the wrong order."
- **Curiosity gap:** "The LinkedIn feature nobody uses that books me 3 calls a week"
- **Giveaway:** "I built a 5-bot AI team that runs my LinkedIn. I'm giving it away."
- **Stat shock:** "88% of founders get $0 at exit. (read this before you raise)"

**Hook rules:**
- Under about 12 words where possible.
- Specific beats vague: "40 calls" beats "lots of calls".
- No clickbait the post doesn't pay off. If the hook promises a system, the post delivers one.
- Always write 3 hook options and pick the strongest.

## 3. Formats ranked by what usually performs

| **Format** | **When to use it** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead magnet post | To get comments, followers and leads | Usually the highest-comment format. A free resource in exchange for a comment |
| Image + text | Frameworks, comparisons, org charts, screenshots | Simple, bold creatives that read in 2 seconds |
| Carousel (PDF) | Step-by-step guides, lists, breakdowns | High dwell time. First slide is the hook |
| Text only | Stories, opinions, lessons | Works when the writing is strong |
| Video | Talking head, demos, behind the scenes | Keep it short with captions on screen |
| Poll | Light engagement | Use rarely. Low value for leads |

## 4. Lead magnet posts (the main format)

- **The offer:** a genuinely useful free resource. That could be a guide, template, prompt pack, system, checklist or swipe file.
- **The mechanic:** "Comment KEYWORD and I'll send it." The owner (or a tool) then DMs the resource to everyone who comments.
- **The keyword goes on the creative only,** in a big, high-contrast button at the bottom. It never goes in the post copy.
- **Make the resource specific.** "The 8-agent Claude system I use to book calls" beats "my tips for LinkedIn".
- **Show what's inside.** 3 to 5 lines or ✅ bullets of what they get makes people want it before they comment.
- **Deliver what you promised.** The resource must exist and match the post before the post goes live.
- **Asking people to connect or follow** so you can DM them is fine. Keep it to one line.
- **Mix it up.** Not every post should be a lead magnet. Roughly 1 in 3 is a good starting ratio. Adjust based on the owner's results.

## 5. Creative (image) rules

- **Size:** 1080x1350 (portrait). It takes up the most space in the mobile feed.
- **Readable on a phone:** big headline, few words, high contrast.
- **One idea per creative.** If it needs explaining, it's too complex.
- **What works:**
  - Org charts of AI agents or teams.
  - Before vs after or "don't vs do" comparisons.
  - Frameworks and tables.
  - Real screenshots of results.
  - Native-looking UI (a chat window, a dashboard, a document).
- **Brand-consistent:** the same fonts and colours every time, so the audience recognises the owner's posts.
- **Never edit real screenshots** or change real numbers in proof images.
- **Check before handing off:** no cut-off text, no overlapping elements, nothing blurry.

## 6. Copy rules

- **Write like a person talking to one person.** Plain words. Short sentences.
- **Lines of 1 to 2 sentences,** with a blank line between most of them.
- **No walls of text.** If a paragraph is more than 3 lines, break it up.
- **Match the owner's voice** (see voice.md). If in doubt, simpler and shorter.
- **Ideal length:** 150 to 300 words for most posts. Lead magnets can run longer if every line earns its place.
- **Emojis:** use sparingly and only if the owner does. Arrows (→) and ✅ bullets are fine for structure.
- **Hashtags:** 0 to 3 at most, or none. They add little reach.
- **Banned phrases** (they sound like AI): "game-changer", "unlock", "supercharge", "revolutionize", "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", "here's the thing", "it's not X, it's Y" used repeatedly, "delve", "leverage" (as a verb), "unleash", "elevate", "navigate the landscape". Also avoid starting with "I'm excited to share".

## 7. Truth and claims (non-negotiable)

- **Only use real numbers and results** that are in business.md or the owner's own data.
- **Never invent:** clients, testimonials, revenue figures, follower counts, screenshots or quotes.
- **Third-party stats** must name their source. If a source can't be found, leave the stat out.
- **Flag anything uncertain** to the owner instead of guessing.
- **Don't copy other creators' posts word for word.** Borrow the format, structure and angle, never the text.
- **Don't name clients** unless the owner confirms it's allowed.

## 8. Posting and engagement

- **Cadence:** 3 to 5 posts a week is a strong default. Consistency matters more than volume.
- **Timing:** weekday mornings in the audience's main time zone usually perform best (roughly 7 to 10am). Test and use the owner's own data.
- **The first hour:**
  - The owner should be available to reply to every comment.
  - Replies count as engagement and keep the post moving.
- **Reply properly:** a real sentence, not just "thanks!". Ask a follow-up question where it fits.
- **Lead magnet comments:** send the resource by DM within a few hours. Slow delivery kills trust and future engagement.
- **No engagement pods** or fake engagement. They can hurt reach and credibility.
- **Don't edit a post heavily in its first hour.**

## 9. What to learn from data

When the Researcher and Ideator review performance, look at:

| **Signal** | **What it tells you** |
| --- | --- |
| Comments | Whether the post started a conversation or the offer was wanted |
| Comments ÷ impressions | How compelling the hook and offer were, regardless of reach |
| Reposts | Whether people found it useful enough to share |
| Impressions | How far the algorithm pushed it |
| Followers gained | Whether new people wanted more from the owner |
| DMs and calls booked | Whether content is turning into business (the number that matters most) |

**Rules for learning:**
- The owner's own top posts are the best data. Replicate what worked for them first.
- Compare like with like: lead magnets against lead magnets, stories against stories.
- One great post can be luck. A pattern across 3 or more posts is a signal.
- Log every rejected post and the reason, and don't repeat the mistake.

## 10. The team's rules

1. Nothing is published or scheduled without the owner's APPROVE.
2. Real numbers only.
3. The owner's own best posts outrank everyone else's.
4. The comment keyword goes on the creative, never in the copy.
5. Every hook gets 3 options.
6. Every post is readable on a phone in under 60 seconds.
7. Borrow formats, never copy words.
8. Each Bot does only its own job and hands off in order: Researcher → Ideator → Copywriter → Designer → Publisher.
9. When unsure, ask the owner. Don't guess.
10. Keep it simple. If a post or creative needs explaining, simplify it.
